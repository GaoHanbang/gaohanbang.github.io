Teaching has been one of the most rewarding parts during my PhD. Across an engineering grande école (École Centrale de Nantes) and a technical institute (IUT de Nantes, Nantes Université), in both French and English, I have come to enjoy meeting students wherever they start from, learning new things alongside them, and sharpening my own teaching as I go. Watching students grow more confident and capable gives me a sense of accomplishment that mirrors what I find in research.

Courses taught

Mechanical Design & CAD — Conception de Mécanique / CAO

ECN & IUT de Nantes Undergraduate → Master FR / EN CATIA V5 (2013) 3DEXPERIENCE (2024)

I have taught computer-aided design across two generations of tooling — from CATIA V5 through to the 3DEXPERIENCE platform — covering part and assembly modelling, end-to-end digital engineering workflows (modelling → simulation → validation), and equipment design with applications to robotics. The course mixes lectures with hands-on tutorials, guiding students from first sketches to consistent, industry-ready digital models.

Computer Science — Informatique

IUT de Nantes Undergraduate FR Excel VBA Programming

An introduction to computing for engineering students: spreadsheet modelling in Excel, automation with VBA, and the fundamentals of programming. The emphasis is on building practical, transferable skills that students can apply directly in technical and project work.

Robotics & Simulation — Robotique et Simulation

ECN & IUT de Nantes Undergraduate → Master FR / EN KUKA · FANUC · UR Robot modelling

Closest to my research, this strand covers the modelling and simulation of industrial robots and production lines, with hands-on experiments on KUKA, FANUC, and Universal Robots (UR) hardware. Students move from kinematic modelling to programming and operating real robot cells safely — a natural bridge between classroom theory and the physical human–robot interaction questions I study in my research.

Whether students come from an engineering school or a technical institute, I try to meet them where they are and send them away a little more capable — and a little more curious — than before.
